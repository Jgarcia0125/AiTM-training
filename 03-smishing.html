CH.03 // FIELD MANUAL

The seven signals in every smish.

Every smishing text contains one or more of the following. If you spot two, treat it as hostile. If you spot three, don't even open it — screenshot, report, delete.

01
Shortened or oddly-named URLs
Links like bit.ly/xyz123, tinyurl.com/xxx, or random domains like ups-track-9847.co hide the real destination. Legit companies use their own domain — ups.com/track/, not a shortener.
02
Impersonal openings
"Dear customer," "Hello user," or no greeting at all. Real companies you have accounts with usually know your name. Generic openers are almost always mass-mailed scams.
03
Urgency about money or delivery
"Your package is held," "Your account will be closed," "Payment failed — update now." These four templates power 80% of smishing. The urgency exists to prevent verification.
04
Requests for personal or financial info
No legitimate business asks for your SSN, password, or full card number over SMS. Ever. Banks especially will never text you to "verify" login details.
05
Odd sender formatting
Texts from long email-style addresses, weird international numbers, or numbers with just 5 digits when you're not expecting an alert — all worth extra scrutiny. Banks use specific short codes; scammers often use throwaway numbers.
06
Reference to something you didn't do
"Your Amazon order for $487," "Your Netflix payment failed" — when you didn't order anything or don't have Netflix. Attackers mass-send these; sometimes they hit lucky.
07
Emotional or authority framing
"IRS Notice," "Court appearance required," "Your account has been suspended." Authority is used to bypass your judgment. Real government agencies (IRS, SSA, DMV) do not text you. Ever.
CH.04 // STANDING ORDERS

Your standing orders.

When a suspicious text lands on your phone — at any hour, from any sender — run this checklist. It takes less time than reading the message.

01
DON'T CLICK
If in doubt, don't tap the link. Verifying takes 30 seconds; a compromise takes 30 days to clean up.
02
GO DIRECT
If it's about your account, open the company's official app or type the URL yourself. Never through the SMS link.
03
REPORT IT
Forward suspicious texts to 7726 (that spells SPAM). Your carrier uses this to block the sender network-wide.
04
DELETE + BLOCK
Once reported, delete the message and block the sender. Don't reply "STOP" — that just confirms the number is active.
05
WATCH FOR PATTERNS
If you get a smish about your work account, tell IT. Attackers often target multiple employees at once.
06
STAY SKEPTICAL
Even a text that looks legit deserves a beat of thought. Especially the ones that don't feel like scams — the best attackers write in plain, professional English.
